One status field can bury a critical alert

ServiceNow scores every alert like an odometer. The leading digits come from the business services the alert affects. A service instance counts only when its Operational Status is Operational. If it isn't, those leading digits read zero, and the incident can inherit the damage too.

Alert score formula, from ServiceNow's alert priority documentation: services × 1,000,000 + severity × 100,000 + CI type × 100 + role × 10 + secondary count + state × 0.001. The Alerts list shows the score divided by 1,000. Services value is the sum of each impacted service's mapped criticality (1 - most critical = 4 points down to 4 - not critical = 1). Only operational Service Instance classes (cmdb_ci_service_auto and extensions) take part in impact calculation.

Incident urgency follows the out-of-box "Calculate Values (Based on the Alert)" flow action: Critical or Major becomes 1 - High, Minor or Warning becomes 2 - Medium. Priority uses the out-of-box impact × urgency lookup. Out of the box, that flow action doesn't set impact, so the impact options reflect common configurations. Pick the one that matches your instance.

Severity, role, and CI type point values are illustrative. Your values live in em_alert_priority_category_mapping and em_alert_priority_ci_type. The alert priority group isn't shown because it's percentile-based across all open alerts, so it can't be computed for a single alert in isolation.
